**Date:** 2026-07-31

Covers the residential referral-origin services. They share most of a process, so
this is one shared baseline plus deltas — the same base + overlay pattern used
for jurisdictions.

Carried over:

- **All three come by referral.** No canvassing, no solicitation-cancellation
  requirement — that applies only to storm restoration
- Covenant self-performs carpentry, trim, and cabinetry; everything else is subbed
- Permitting is handled in-house
- Invoicing at the end of each phase
- Selections locked before permit submittal

## The thing all three have that new construction doesn't

**You are building against conditions you cannot see.**

New construction starts with a clean lot and known materials. These three start
with someone else's work, often decades old, frequently undocumented, sometimes
not to any code that was ever in force. Behind that wall could be a bearing
condition nobody drew, failed plumbing, aluminium wiring, or no header at all.

The whole baseline is shaped around that: an explicit discovery phase before
pricing is fixed, a written allowance for the unknown, and a change order
mechanism that's agreed **before** anyone opens a wall rather than negotiated
in the hallway once something is found.

**Two compliance items that follow from working on old buildings:**

**Lead paint — pre-1978.** Federal EPA Renovation, Repair and Painting rule
requires certified firms and lead-safe work practices when disturbing painted
surfaces in housing or child-occupied facilities built before 1978. Vero Beach
has plenty of that stock. Certification, containment, and record-keeping are
required, and the fines are per-day. **Confirm Covenant's RRP certification is
current before any pre-1978 job.**

**Asbestos.** Common in flooring, mastic, popcorn ceilings, pipe insulation, and
sheet materials in older buildings. Commercial demolition and renovation carry
survey and notification obligations under NESHAP administered through Florida
DEP. Residential is less strictly regulated but the liability is real.
**Survey before demo on anything old enough to warrant it, and do not let a sub
"just take it out."**

Neither of these is legal advice and both need verifying against current
requirements.

## Shared baseline — 6 stages

### Stage 1 — Referral, consultation, and feasibility
*1–2 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We visit, listen, and tell you honestly what's possible in your space and budget.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Site visit · scope conversation · rough budget range · honest feasibility read |
| You provide | Access · your goals and budget · how you'll use the space during work |

**Billing:** None, or a paid design retainer where design work begins.

**Internal checklist:**
- Record the referral source in the CRM — **referral is your entire lead channel
  for these three services and it is currently untracked**
- Walk the space; photograph existing conditions before anything is agreed
- Identify obvious unknowns — age of structure, visible modifications, prior
  unpermitted work
- **Establish build year.** Pre-1978 triggers lead-safe requirements; older
  buildings raise asbestos questions
- Check for prior permits on the property; unpermitted existing work becomes
  your problem once you pull a permit
- Give a range, never a number, before discovery is done
- Set expectations on living/operating in the space during construction

**Exit criteria:** scope understood · build year known · budget range accepted ·
client wants to proceed

---

### Stage 2 — Discovery and design
*3–10 weeks* · `performedBy: mixed`

**Public summary:** We investigate what's actually behind the walls, then design to it — and lock every selection before we submit for permit.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Exploratory investigation · measured drawings · design · engineering where needed · selections guidance |
| You provide | Decisions at each revision · **all selections, signed off** · access for investigation |

**Billing:** Invoice at end of stage.

**Internal checklist:**
- **Exploratory openings where structure or systems are in question.** An hour
  with a drywall saw now is worth weeks of change orders later
- Field-measure everything; never design off original drawings for an old building
- Identify bearing walls, structural modifications, and system capacity
- Assess electrical panel capacity, plumbing condition, HVAC adequacy
- Engineering where structure is altered
- **Lead and asbestos assessment where age warrants**
- Determine whether a permit is required for the actual scope
- **Selections signed off in writing** — full list, same discipline as new builds
- Millwork shop drawings started off signed selections
- **Written allowance for concealed conditions**, agreed in the contract with a
  defined change order process
- Cap revision rounds; beyond that is a change order

**Exit criteria:** existing conditions understood · design complete · **all
selections signed** · concealed-conditions allowance agreed · permit path known

---

### Stage 3 — Contract and permitting
*2–8 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We put it in writing and handle the permit ourselves.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Contract · permit submittal · plan review responses · NOC recording · scheduling |
| You provide | Signed contract · deposit · owner signatures · access decisions |

**Billing:** Deposit at signing; invoice at end of stage.

**Internal checklist:**
- Construction lien law disclosure — Ch. 713
- Homeowner's Construction Recovery Fund notice where applicable — § 489.1425
- **Concealed-conditions allowance and change order process stated in the
  contract**, not assumed
- Run the submittal checklist before filing
- **Record the NOC and post before first inspection** — § 713.13
- Track plan review comments; log dates so real turnaround becomes measurable
- COIs, signed subcontracts, licence verification before any sub mobilises
- Confirm general liability active; builder's risk where scope warrants
- **Protection plan agreed** — floor protection, dust containment, access route,
  working hours, where the client's belongings go

**Exit criteria:** signed contract · permit issued where required · NOC recorded
· subs contracted and insured · protection plan agreed

---

### Stage 4 — Demolition and rough work
*2–8 weeks* · `performedBy: subcontractor`

**Public summary:** The old comes out, the new structure and systems go in.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Containment · demolition · structural work · rough-ins · QC at each hold point |
| You provide | Cleared space · access · decisions on anything discovered |

**Billing:** Invoice at end of stage.

**QC hold points:**
- **Containment and protection installed before demolition starts** — this is
  what clients remember, and dust in the rest of an occupied house is the fastest
  way to lose a referral source
- **Lead-safe practices where pre-1978**; asbestos cleared or abated before demo
- Photograph everything exposed during demo — **this is the discovery record and
  the basis of any change order**
- **Anything discovered gets photographed, priced, and issued as a written change
  order before work continues.** Do not proceed on a verbal
- Structural work inspected before cover
- Rough electrical, plumbing, mechanical inspected before cover
- Existing systems tied into new work verified, not assumed

**Sub admin:** partial lien releases with every payment

**Exit criteria:** rough inspections passed · discovered conditions resolved by
change order · site clean

---

### Stage 5 — Finishes and millwork
*3–12 weeks* · `performedBy: mixed`

**Public summary:** Everything you actually see — including cabinetry we build ourselves.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Drywall, paint, flooring, tile · **in-house cabinetry, trim, and finish carpentry** · fixtures and appliances |
| You provide | Access · walkthrough at key points |

**Billing:** Invoice at end of stage. Allowance overages billed as incurred.

**Subbed — QC hold points:**
- Insulation and drywall after rough inspections pass
- Tile and flooring substrate verified flat and sound before finish goes down
- Paint, fixtures, appliances, final trim-out by trade

**Self-performed — millwork production:**
- Shop drawings finalised against **field measurements taken after rough work**,
  not against design dimensions
- Material ordered against signed selections from stage 2
- Shop fabrication scheduled to land after substrate is ready
- Cabinet installation — level, scribe, hardware
- Interior trim, doors, casing, base
- **Countertop template only after cabinets are set** — templating early is the
  most common cause of a remake
- **Covenant punches its own work before the client sees it**

**Exit criteria:** finishes complete · millwork installed and punched · space
clean

---

### Stage 6 — Final inspection, punch, and handover
*1–3 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** Final inspections, punch list, and we hand the space back.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Final inspections · punch · warranty documentation · final clean |
| You provide | Walkthrough attendance · final payment |

**Billing:** Final invoice against complete lien release package.

**Internal checklist:**
- Internal punch **before** the client walkthrough
- Final inspections per permit scope
- Client walkthrough; punch list written, dated, and resolved
- Warranty package, manuals, care instructions for millwork and finishes
- **Reconcile every Notice to Owner against a matching final lien release**
- **Contractor's final payment affidavit** — § 713.06
- Final clean
- **Request a review and ask for referrals by name.** These three services live
  entirely on referral; the walkthrough is the highest-goodwill moment you will
  ever have with this client and it is currently unused
- Portfolio photos with written permission

**Exit criteria:** inspections passed · punch signed off · lien releases complete
· final payment received · review requested

**Authority:** § 713.06 · FBC 110

## Delta — custom kitchens and cabinetry

*The service closest to the founder's origin, and the one with the most
self-performed content.*

**Where it differs:**

- **Stage 5 dominates.** Most of the value is millwork Covenant builds. Shop
  capacity, not sub availability, is the schedule constraint
- **Permit is scope-dependent.** Cabinet replacement in the same footprint often
  needs none; moving plumbing, gas, or electrical usually does. **Decide this in
  stage 2 and document the reasoning** — "we didn't think it needed one" is not a
  defence
- **The client has no kitchen.** Temporary kitchen setup, realistic downtime
  estimate, and a schedule that treats appliance delivery as a hard date
- **Appliance specs before cabinet fabrication.** Cabinets are built around
  actual appliance dimensions; a substituted model after fabrication is a remake
- **Countertop template after cabinet set** — never before
- Additional stage 2 checks: panel capacity for new appliance loads, existing
  plumbing condition, ventilation routing and make-up air

**Typical duration:** 8–16 weeks from contract, shop capacity permitting.

## Delta — remodels and renovations

*The highest-variance service. Discovery risk is the whole story.*

**Where it differs:**

- **Stage 2 discovery is non-negotiable.** Exploratory openings before a fixed
  price. The alternative is pricing a guess and arguing about it later
- **Concealed-conditions allowance is essential**, not optional. Name a number in
  the contract and a written process for exceeding it
- **Permit triggers code upgrades.** Opening a wall can pull in current-code
  requirements for electrical, egress, insulation, or smoke alarms that the
  client did not budget for. **Flag this in stage 2, before contract** — it is
  the most common source of a blindsided client
- **Unpermitted prior work becomes yours** the moment you pull a permit on the
  property. Check permit history in stage 1
- **Phasing where the client stays in the house.** Which rooms, in what order,
  and what stays functional throughout
- Pre-1978 lead requirements apply most often here

**Typical duration:** 10–24 weeks, heavily scope-dependent.

## Delta — commercial projects *(summary only — see `process-commercial.md`)*

*Different code, different reviewers, different clock. Retained here as a
one-page orientation; the working process lives in its own file.*

**Where it differs:**

- **FBC-Building, not Residential.** Different requirements throughout — egress,
  occupancy load, fire separation, structural
- **Accessibility.** ADA and the Florida Accessibility Code apply. Alterations
  can trigger path-of-travel obligations well beyond the work area — **this
  surprises people and it is expensive to discover late**
- **Fire marshal review** in addition to building department, typically with its
  own timeline
- **Health department** where food service is involved
- **Landlord coordination on tenant improvements.** Lease terms, landlord
  approval, base-building conditions, who owns the improvements at lease end
- **The client is losing money while closed.** Schedule pressure is financial,
  not preferential. Phased or after-hours work is common and should be priced as
  such
- **Certificate of Occupancy or Certificate of Completion** depending on whether
  occupancy classification changes
- Prevailing wage and bonding on any public work

**Additional stage 2 checks:** occupancy classification, occupant load, egress
capacity, accessibility path of travel, fire sprinkler and alarm impact, grease
and ventilation where applicable.

**Typical duration:** 12–30 weeks. Fire marshal and accessibility review are the
usual long poles.

## Open items

1. **Is Covenant's EPA RRP lead certification current?** Required for pre-1978
   work, and Vero Beach has a lot of it.
2. **Asbestos protocol.** Who surveys, who abates, and at what age threshold do
   you test?
3. **Concealed-conditions allowance.** Do you carry one now? What number, and how
   is it presented to the client?
4. **Shop capacity.** How many kitchens can run concurrently? This is the real
   constraint on the kitchens business and it should drive the duration ranges.
5. **Commercial mix.** Mostly tenant improvements, or ground-up? Changes how much
   of the commercial delta matters.
6. **Referral tracking.** Where do these referrals actually come from — past
   clients, designers, realtors, mitigation partners? Worth knowing before
   spending anything on marketing.
7. **Verify lead, asbestos, and accessibility requirements** against current
   rules before treating this as authoritative.
