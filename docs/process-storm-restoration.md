# Process template — storm restoration (Florida baseline)

**Status:** draft. Not reviewed. **Contains compliance items that need a lawyer.**
**Date:** 2026-07-31

Confirmed by founder:

- **Covenant's scope is reconstruction.** Mitigation — water extraction, drying,
  mold remediation, emergency make-safe — is referred to a mitigation contractor
- **Restoration work comes from door-to-door canvassing**
- **All other services come by referral**
- Covenant self-performs carpentry, trim, and cabinetry; everything else is subbed
- Permitting is handled in-house
- Statewide Florida baseline; municipality detail is a per-job overlay

---

## Read this before anything else

Storm restoration is **not new construction with a different client**. Three
things make it a different business, and all three carry legal exposure that
custom homes don't.

**1. You cannot adjust the claim.** Florida licenses public adjusting
separately. A contractor may describe and price *their own scope of work* to a
carrier. Negotiating or settling the claim on the homeowner's behalf is public
adjusting, and doing it unlicensed is a regulatory problem — not a grey area.

**2. You cannot touch the deductible.** Florida prohibits a contractor from
offering to pay, waive, rebate, or absorb an insurance deductible as an
inducement — and prohibits advertising it. This is the most common way
restoration contractors get disciplined.

**3. Door-knocking triggers a cancellation right.** Contracts signed at the
homeowner's residence following in-person solicitation carry a statutory right
to cancel within three business days, with required written notice. **You canvass
door-to-door, so this applies to essentially every restoration contract you
sign.** A contract missing that notice is not enforceable the way you'd expect.

**None of the above is legal advice, and the statute references below are drafted
from general Florida practice.** Budget an hour with a construction attorney
before this becomes the SOP of record or any of it reaches the website.

## The scope boundary — and the dependency it creates

Covenant rebuilds. Someone else dries.

```
Loss occurs
   │
   ├─► Mitigation contractor          ├─► Covenant
   │   • emergency make-safe          │   • documented reconstruction scope
   │   • water extraction             │   • permitting
   │   • structural drying            │   • rebuild
   │   • mold remediation             │   • supplements on our scope
   │                                   │
   └─── pre-mitigation photos ────────►│  ← the dependency
        drying logs, moisture data      │
```

**This boundary creates the single biggest documentation risk in the file.**

By the time you arrive, the property has been altered. Wet drywall is cut out,
carpet is gone, contents are packed. **The only record of the loss in its
original condition belongs to the mitigation company**, and you need it — for the
adjuster meeting, and much more so for supplements.

If you can't produce evidence of what was there before mitigation touched it, a
carrier can characterise your scope as unsupported. So "obtain the mitigation
file" is not an administrative nicety; it is a gate in stage 2.

**The upside of the same boundary:** a mitigation contractor who trusts you sends
you every rebuild they touch. That's a warm, high-intent, pre-qualified lead
channel with a claim already open — far better than a cold door knock. Worth
formalising as a relationship, not leaving to chance.

## What makes this process structurally different

| New home builds | Storm restoration |
|---|---|
| Client controls the money | **Carrier controls the money** |
| Scope is designed | **Scope is negotiated, then discovered** |
| Schedule set by permit + trades | Gated by adjuster availability |
| Photos are marketing | **Photos are the product** — they justify payment |
| Billing at end of each phase | Billing follows carrier disbursement |
| Referral origin | **Canvass origin** — cancellation notice required |
| One decision-maker | Homeowner, adjuster, mortgagee |
| You see the site first | **You arrive after mitigation altered it** |

## Two entry points

```
Storm event
   │
   ├── Mitigation partner referral ──┐
   │   claim already open,            │
   │   documentation exists           ├──► Stage 2 onward
   │                                  │
   └── Canvass ──────► Stage 1 ──────┘
       door-to-door
```

A referral from a mitigation contractor arrives warm, with a claim number and a
documentation file. A canvassed lead arrives cold, with neither. Same process
from stage 2, materially different starting position — and the referral path
skips the cancellation-notice requirement, since the contract wasn't signed
following in-person solicitation. **Confirm that reading with counsel.**

## Draft stages

### Stage 1 — Canvass and first contact
*1–3 days* · `performedBy: covenant` · *canvass entry only*

**Public summary:** We inspect storm-damaged properties in affected neighbourhoods and tell you honestly whether you have a claim.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Neighbourhood assessment · free exterior inspection · honest read on whether damage is claimable |
| You provide | Permission to inspect · carrier and policy information if you proceed |

**Billing:** None.

**Internal checklist:**
- **Check local solicitation ordinances before canvassing.** Several Treasure
  Coast municipalities regulate door-to-door and some require a permit. Per-
  jurisdiction item for the overlay
- Confirm the storm date and event; damage must tie to a covered event
- Leave written material with the licence number visible — § 489.119
- **Do not state or imply the deductible can be waived, absorbed, or covered.**
  Not verbally, not on a flyer, not "we'll take care of it"
- **Do not promise a claim will be approved.** You don't decide that
- **If there's active water intrusion or standing water, refer to mitigation
  immediately.** Damage compounds by the hour, and a homeowner who watches you
  do nothing about it while you talk about rebuilding does not sign
- Log the address and outcome in the CRM whether or not they engage — canvass
  data is a real asset and it's currently going nowhere

**Exit criteria:** homeowner consents to a documented inspection · mitigation
referred if needed

**Authority:** § 489.119 · § 489.147 · local solicitation ordinances

---

### Stage 2 — Inspection and damage documentation
*1–3 days* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We document the damage thoroughly, because what gets documented is what gets paid.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Full exterior and interior inspection · photo and measurement documentation · written reconstruction scope · collecting the mitigation file |
| You provide | Interior access · prior repair history · mitigation company contact · any existing damage you know of |

**Billing:** None.

**Internal checklist — this stage carries the job:**

*Obtain from the mitigation contractor — do not proceed without trying:*
- **Pre-mitigation photographs** — the only record of original condition
- Moisture readings and drying logs
- Scope of what was removed and why
- Any materials retained as evidence
- Their invoice, so mitigation and reconstruction scopes don't overlap or leave a
  gap between them

*Document yourself:*
- Date-stamped photos: elevations, roof planes, all slopes, penetrations,
  flashing, soft metals, interior ceilings and walls
- **Photograph the current post-mitigation state clearly** — what's been removed
  is as important as what remains
- **Photograph pre-existing and unrelated damage too.** Failing to distinguish it
  is how a whole claim gets denied for misrepresentation
- Measurements and roof diagram
- Written reconstruction scope — materials, quantities, line items
- Note code-upgrade items separately; ordinance-and-law is a distinct coverage
  bucket and gets missed constantly
- **Keep the scope factual and limited to your own work.** Do not characterise
  what the policy covers or what the carrier owes
- Upload everything to the CRM project immediately

**Exit criteria:** mitigation file obtained or its absence documented ·
complete photo set · measured reconstruction scope · uploaded

---

### Stage 3 — Contract and claim coordination
*3–7 days* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** You file your claim. We give you the documentation to support the rebuild.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Providing our documented scope · explaining our work · coordinating inspection timing |
| You provide | **You file the claim with your carrier** · signed contract · deductible acknowledgement |

**Billing:** Deposit per contract, subject to statutory limits.

**Internal checklist:**
- **The homeowner files the claim.** Covenant supplies documentation. Do not file
  or negotiate on their behalf
- If mitigation already opened the claim, get the claim number and adjuster
  contact rather than duplicating
- **Three-business-day right of cancellation in every canvass-originated
  contract**, with the required notice form
- **Contract states the deductible is the homeowner's obligation and will be
  collected.** Never waived, discounted, or absorbed
- **Contract scope is reconstruction only** — state plainly that mitigation is
  separate and separately billed, so the homeowner isn't surprised by two
  invoices
- Construction lien law disclosure — Ch. 713
- Homeowner's Construction Recovery Fund notice — § 489.1425
- Verify the policy is active and the loss date falls within the claim-filing
  window — Florida tightened these deadlines in 2022
- Identify the mortgagee early; on larger losses the lender endorses the check

**Exit criteria:** signed contract with cancellation notice · claim number ·
deductible acknowledged in writing

**Authority:** Ch. 713 · § 489.1425 · § 489.147 · § 626.854 · home solicitation
sale statute · claim-filing deadline statute

---

### Stage 4 — Adjuster meeting and scope agreement
*2–8 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We meet the adjuster on site and walk them through what we documented.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Attending the adjuster inspection · presenting our documented scope · answering questions about our work |
| You provide | Authorisation for us to attend · the carrier's estimate when it arrives |

**Billing:** None at this stage.

**Internal checklist:**
- Attend the adjuster inspection. Being present is the difference between a scope
  built from your documentation and one built from a twenty-minute walkthrough
- **Bring the mitigation file.** The adjuster is pricing a rebuild of something
  they cannot see any more; pre-mitigation photos are what make your scope
  credible
- Present your photos and measurements; walk the damage physically
- **Discuss the scope and cost of your work. Do not argue coverage, policy
  interpretation, or settlement amount**
- Compare the carrier's estimate against your scope line by line
- Log every omission and underpriced line with the photo that supports it — this
  becomes the supplement file
- If the homeowner disputes **coverage** rather than scope, refer them to a
  licensed public adjuster or their own counsel

**Exit criteria:** carrier scope received · line-by-line comparison complete ·
gaps logged with documentation

**Authority:** § 626.854

---

### Stage 5 — Permit and mobilization
*1–4 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We pull the permit and get the crew scheduled.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Permit submittal · NOC recording · scheduling · material ordering |
| You provide | Deductible · carrier payment endorsement · access |

**Billing:** **Deductible collected before work begins.** ACV payment applied.

**Internal checklist:**
- Run the submittal checklist before filing — in-house permitting means an
  incomplete package is a delay with nobody else to chase
- **Record the NOC and post on site before first inspection** — § 713.13
- **Collect the deductible. Hard gate.** Work does not start without it. It is
  not a negotiating tool and cannot be discounted
- **Confirm the structure is dry and mitigation is signed off before rebuilding.**
  Building over a wet substrate is a callback, a mold claim, and a warranty
  problem — get the mitigation contractor's dry certificate in writing
- Confirm ACV payment received and endorsed; involve the mortgagee if named
- Material ordered against the agreed scope, not the hoped-for scope
- COIs, signed subcontracts, licence verification for every trade
- Confirm builder's risk and general liability active

**Exit criteria:** permit issued · NOC recorded and posted · **deductible
collected** · **dry certificate in hand** · subs contracted and insured

**Authority:** § 713.13 · § 489.147 · FBC 105

---

### Stage 6 — Production
*2–8 weeks* · `performedBy: subcontractor` *(millwork in-house)*

**Public summary:** The repairs get built, documented at every step.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Trade coordination · QC at each hold point · progress documentation · discovered-damage reporting |
| You provide | Site access · decisions on upgrades beyond the covered scope |

**Billing:** Progress against carrier disbursement.

**QC hold points:**
- Tear-off photographed **before** anything is covered — discovered damage is
  invisible and unpayable once it's under new material
- Decking, sheathing, substrate condition documented
- **Moisture re-verified before closing any wall**, even with a dry certificate.
  You own the rebuild; you own the callback
- Underlayment and flashing verified before roofing
- Fastener schedule verified against product approval
- **Damage discovered mid-production gets photographed, scoped, and logged the
  same day.** Highest-value supplement material there is, and it evaporates if
  nobody documents it in the moment

**Sub admin:** partial lien releases with every payment · punch verified before
final payment

**Exit criteria:** work complete · inspections passed · discovered-damage file
assembled

---

### Stage 7 — Final inspection and completion
*1–2 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** The city inspects, we punch the job, and you sign off.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Final inspections · punch list · certificate of completion · warranty documentation |
| You provide | Walkthrough attendance · signed completion certificate |

**Billing:** Invoice for completed work.

**Internal checklist:**
- Internal punch before the client walkthrough
- Final building and trade inspections per scope
- Product approvals and material documentation submitted
- **Completion photos matched to the stage 2 damage set** — before-and-after
  pairs are what carriers respond to
- Warranty package and manuals — **state clearly that the warranty covers
  reconstruction, not mitigation work performed by others**
- Signed certificate of completion — required for depreciation release
- Request review and portfolio photo permission

**Exit criteria:** inspections passed · punch signed off · completion
certificate signed

---

### Stage 8 — Supplements, depreciation release, and closeout
*2–12 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We submit documentation for anything discovered during the work, and close the job out.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Supplement documentation for our scope · depreciation release paperwork · final invoicing and lien releases |
| You provide | Carrier correspondence · final payment |

**Billing:** Final invoice against complete lien release package.

**Internal checklist:**
- Assemble the supplement file: discovered damage, omitted line items,
  code-upgrade requirements, price corrections — **each with the photo and
  measurement supporting it**
- **Watch the supplemental claim deadline.** Florida tightened this in 2022 and a
  late supplement is simply lost. Confirm the current window
- Submit supplement documentation for **your scope of work.** Do not negotiate
  the settlement. Mitigation supplements are the mitigation company's to file
- Submit completion certificate to trigger depreciation / RCV release
- Confirm mortgagee endorsement where required
- **Reconcile every Notice to Owner against a matching final lien release**
- **Contractor's final payment affidavit** — § 713.06
- **Confirm the deductible was collected in full.** A written-off deductible is a
  compliance problem, not a discount
- Close the project in the CRM; move portfolio-eligible photos across
- **Thank the referring mitigation contractor.** This is a relationship, and the
  next referral depends on it

**Exit criteria:** supplements resolved · depreciation released · **deductible
collected in full** · complete lien release package · final payment received

**Authority:** § 713.06 · § 489.147 · supplemental claim deadline statute

## Public page — what actually renders

Collapse eight stages to six: merge 1+2 into "Inspection and documentation",
merge 7+8 into "Completion and closeout".

**Say plainly that Covenant rebuilds and does not mitigate.** Setting that
boundary up front prevents the worst call you can get — a homeowner with water
running who thinks you're on the way with equipment. Pair it with "we'll connect
you with a mitigation company we trust," which is genuinely useful and costs
nothing.

**Deliberately not public:** QC hold points, `internalSteps`, `exitCriteria`,
statute citations, supplement strategy, sub admin, billing detail.

**Wording that must appear:**

- Deductibles are the homeowner's responsibility and are collected in full
- The homeowner files and owns the claim; Covenant documents and performs the work
- Covenant does not adjust claims or interpret policy coverage
- Covenant's scope is reconstruction; mitigation is performed by others

**Wording that must never appear:** anything implying you'll handle the
deductible, get the claim approved, fight the insurance company, maximise the
settlement, or work with any carrier. Several are standard in restoration
marketing and several are exactly what draws enforcement in Florida.

Honest differentiators instead: **licensed since 2005**, **CBC licence held
in-house**, **permitting handled in-house**, **documented with photographs at
every stage**, **progress visible in your own portal**.

## Open items

1. **Billing model.** Restoration is gated by carrier disbursement — deductible,
   then ACV, then depreciation on completion. Confirm how you actually invoice
   against that; it can't follow the phase-end pattern.
2. **Which mitigation companies do you refer to?** Naming one or two partners
   makes the referral real rather than aspirational.
3. **Do they refer back?** If not, that's the cheapest business development
   available to you and worth an explicit conversation.
4. **Who attends adjuster meetings** — Josias Sr, Jr, or a designated estimator?
5. **Xactimate.** Do you estimate in it? Carriers work in it, and matching their
   format materially changes how supplements land.
6. **Cancellation notice on referral jobs.** A mitigation referral isn't in-person
   solicitation. Confirm with counsel whether the notice is still required.
7. **Verify every statute reference** with counsel before use.
