# Process template — commercial projects (Florida baseline)

**Status:** draft. Not reviewed.
**Date:** 2026-07-31

Carried over:

- **Referral origin.** No canvassing, no solicitation-cancellation requirement
- Covenant self-performs carpentry, trim, and cabinetry; everything else is subbed
- Permitting is handled in-house
- Invoicing at the end of each phase
- Selections locked before permit submittal
- Statewide Florida baseline; municipality detail is a per-job overlay

---

## Why commercial gets its own file

Commercial is not "a bigger remodel." Four structural differences change the
process rather than just the scale.

**1. Different code entirely.** FBC-Building, not FBC-Residential. Egress,
occupancy load, fire separation, and structural requirements are all different,
and the analysis has to happen before design — not during permitting.

**2. Multiple parallel reviewers.** Building department, fire marshal, and often
health department review at the same time, on different clocks, with the power
to contradict each other. A residential permit is one queue. A commercial permit
is three, and the job moves at the speed of the slowest.

**3. Accessibility obligations that extend past the work area.** Alterations can
trigger path-of-travel requirements — accessible route, restrooms, drinking
fountains, signage — for areas you never intended to touch. Federal ADA and the
Florida Accessibility Code both apply. **This is the single most common source of
a blindsided commercial client**, and it surfaces at plan review when the budget
is already set.

**4. Downtime is money.** A homeowner living through a remodel is inconvenienced.
A business closed during construction is losing revenue daily. Schedule pressure
is financial, which changes how work gets phased, when crews run, and what
premium the client will pay to compress.

Add a fifth for tenant improvements: **you have two clients.** The tenant signs
your contract; the landlord controls the building and often approves the plans.

---

## Two variants

```
Commercial
   │
   ├── Tenant improvement ──► lease-driven, landlord approval gate,
   │                           existing base building, occupancy may change
   │
   └── Ground-up ───────────► site plan approval, civil engineering,
                               stormwater, parking, landscaping, utilities
```

They share stages 3 onward. Stages 1 and 2 differ enough to branch — a TI starts
with a lease and a landlord; a ground-up starts with entitlement and civil work
that can take longer than the building itself.

## Draft stages

### Stage 1 — Referral, consultation, and due diligence
*2–4 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We walk the space, understand your operation, and tell you honestly what's possible.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Site visit · operational needs conversation · preliminary code read · rough budget range · feasibility |
| You provide | Access · lease or property documents · business timeline · how you'll operate during work |

**Billing:** None, or a paid feasibility retainer.

**Internal checklist:**
- Record the referral source in the CRM
- Photograph existing conditions before anything is agreed
- **Establish current occupancy classification and whether the intended use
  changes it.** A classification change drives fire separation, egress,
  sprinklers, parking, and whether you need a new CO rather than a completion
  certificate. Get this wrong early and the budget is wrong
- Occupant load and egress capacity for the intended use
- **Preliminary accessibility read** — existing restrooms, entrance, path of
  travel, parking. Flag likely obligations now, not at plan review
- Existing systems: electrical service capacity, HVAC tonnage, plumbing, whether
  fire sprinkler and alarm exist
- Check permit history; unpermitted prior work becomes your problem
- Build year — lead and asbestos considerations on older stock
- Give a range, never a number

*Tenant improvement adds:*
- **Read the lease.** Work letter, landlord approval process, permitted use,
  who owns improvements at lease end, restoration obligations, allowance amount
  and how it's disbursed
- Landlord's insurance and contractor requirements — often higher limits,
  additional insured, waiver of subrogation
- Base building conditions and what the landlord is responsible for
- **Confirm the tenant's lease commencement date.** It is usually the real
  deadline and it is usually not negotiable

*Ground-up adds:*
- Zoning, permitted use, setbacks, height, parking ratio
- Site plan approval requirement and timeline — **frequently longer than
  construction**
- Utility availability and capacity
- Stormwater, drainage, and any water management district permitting
- Traffic or concurrency review where applicable

**Exit criteria:** occupancy classification known · accessibility exposure
flagged · lease reviewed (TI) or entitlement path known (ground-up) · budget
range accepted

### Stage 2 — Design, code analysis, and selections
*8–20 weeks* · `performedBy: mixed`

**Public summary:** Sealed drawings, a full code analysis, and every selection locked before we submit.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Architect and engineer coordination · code and accessibility analysis · systems design · selections guidance |
| You provide | Operational requirements · equipment specifications · **all selections, signed off** · landlord approval where required |

**Billing:** Invoice at end of stage.

**Internal checklist:**
- **Commercial work generally requires sealed drawings by a Florida-licensed
  architect or engineer.** Confirm the design professional early; this is not
  optional the way it sometimes is residentially
- Written code analysis: occupancy classification, construction type, allowable
  area and height, egress, fire separation, sprinkler requirement
- **Accessibility analysis with path-of-travel scope defined and priced.** Put a
  number on it before contract
- MEP design — electrical load calculation, HVAC sizing, plumbing fixture count
  per occupancy
- Fire sprinkler and alarm design where required; these are deferred submittals
  in many jurisdictions and have their own review
- Energy code compliance for commercial
- Equipment cut sheets from the client — **kitchen, medical, and industrial
  equipment drives MEP design and cannot be substituted later without rework**
- **Selections signed off in writing**
- Millwork shop drawings started off signed selections
- **Landlord plan approval obtained in writing before permit submittal** (TI)
- Civil, site, and landscape drawings (ground-up)

**Exit criteria:** sealed drawings complete · code and accessibility analysis
written · **all selections signed** · **landlord approval in hand (TI)** ·
deferred submittals identified

---

### Stage 3 — Contract, permitting, and parallel reviews
*6–16 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** We put it in writing and run the permit through building, fire, and health review ourselves.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Contract · permit submittal · building, fire marshal, and health review · NOC recording · scheduling |
| You provide | Signed contract · deposit · owner or landlord signatures · impact fee payment |

**Billing:** Deposit at signing; invoice at end of stage.

**Internal checklist:**
- Construction lien law disclosure — Ch. 713
- **Concealed-conditions allowance and change order process stated in the
  contract**
- **Liquidated damages and schedule terms.** Commercial clients frequently want
  them; understand the exposure before signing
- Payment and retainage terms — commercial contracts commonly hold retainage
- Bonding where required — public work, or landlord requirement
- Run the submittal checklist before filing
- **Submit to building, fire marshal, and health simultaneously where the
  jurisdiction allows.** Sequential submission adds weeks for no reason
- **Track each reviewer separately.** They comment independently and can
  conflict; resolving a contradiction between fire and building is your job
- Deferred submittals — sprinkler, alarm, hood suppression — filed on their own
  timeline
- **Record the NOC and post before first inspection** — § 713.13
- Pay impact fees; commercial impact fees are substantially higher than
  residential and can surprise a client who budgeted from a residential frame
- COIs, subcontracts, licence verification — **commercial insurance limits are
  typically higher, and landlord or owner requirements may exceed your standard**
- Confirm builder's risk and general liability meet contract and lease
  requirements
- Protection plan where the business stays operational — containment, dust,
  noise, access route, working hours, life-safety during construction

**Exit criteria:** signed contract · **all permits issued** · NOC recorded · subs
contracted and insured to required limits · protection plan agreed

**Authority:** Ch. 713 · § 713.13 · FBC 105 · FBC-B

---

### Stage 4 — Demolition, site, and structural work
*3–12 weeks* · `performedBy: subcontractor`

**Public summary:** The old comes out, the structure and site work go in.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Containment · demolition · structural work · site and civil work · QC at each hold point |
| You provide | Cleared space · access · decisions on anything discovered |

**Billing:** Invoice at end of stage.

**QC hold points:**
- **Containment and life-safety protection before demolition** where the building
  stays occupied — egress must remain compliant throughout construction, and the
  fire marshal can and will check
- **Asbestos survey and clearance before demolition.** Commercial demolition and
  renovation carry survey and notification obligations under NESHAP through
  Florida DEP. Do not let a sub "just take it out"
- Lead-safe practices where the building predates 1978 and the use warrants
- Photograph everything exposed during demo — the discovery record and the basis
  of any change order
- **Anything discovered gets photographed, priced, and issued as a written change
  order before work continues.** Never proceed on a verbal
- Structural work inspected before cover
- Site work, underground utilities, and stormwater inspected (ground-up)

**Sub admin:** partial lien releases with every payment

**Exit criteria:** demolition complete and cleared · structural inspections
passed · discovered conditions resolved by change order

---

### Stage 5 — Systems and rough-ins
*4–12 weeks* · `performedBy: subcontractor`

**Public summary:** Everything behind the walls — power, plumbing, air, and life safety.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Electrical, plumbing, mechanical rough-in · fire sprinkler and alarm · trade sequencing · QC |
| You provide | Final equipment specifications · access |

**Billing:** Invoice at end of stage.

**QC hold points:**
- Sequence trades so rough-ins pass **before** anything is covered
- **Fire sprinkler and alarm rough with their own inspections** — these are
  separate from building inspections and frequently the schedule constraint
- Electrical service and panel work verified against the load calculation
- HVAC ductwork and equipment verified against design, not against habit
- Plumbing fixture rough matched to the accessibility layout — **an accessible
  restroom roughed at residential dimensions is a demolition-and-redo**
- Grease interceptor, hood, and suppression where food service is involved
- Above-ceiling inspection before ceiling grid closes
- Energy code inspection where applicable

**Sub admin:** lien releases current across every trade

**Exit criteria:** all rough inspections passed including fire · above-ceiling
signed off

---

### Stage 6 — Finishes and millwork
*4–12 weeks* · `performedBy: mixed`

**Public summary:** Everything you and your customers actually see — including millwork we build ourselves.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Drywall, paint, flooring, ceilings · **in-house millwork, casework, and trim** · fixtures and equipment set |
| You provide | Access · equipment delivery coordination · walkthrough at key points |

**Billing:** Invoice at end of stage. Allowance overages billed as incurred.

**Subbed — QC hold points:**
- Drywall and ceiling grid after rough inspections pass
- **Fire-rated assemblies built to the tested detail** — a rated wall finished
  wrong fails inspection and is expensive to correct after finishes
- Flooring substrate verified before finish goes down
- Paint, ceilings, fixtures, and final trim-out by trade

**Self-performed — millwork production:**
- Shop drawings finalised against **field measurements taken after rough work**
- Material ordered against signed selections from stage 2
- **Commercial millwork carries different expectations** — durability, fire
  rating where required, accessibility at counters and transaction surfaces
- Fabrication scheduled to land after substrate is ready
- Casework installation — level, scribe, hardware, anchorage
- **Countertop and transaction-surface heights verified against accessibility
  requirements** before template
- **Covenant punches its own work before the client sees it**

**Exit criteria:** finishes complete · millwork installed and punched ·
accessible dimensions verified

---

### Stage 7 — Inspections, certificate, and turnover
*2–6 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** Building and fire sign off, the certificate issues, and you open.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Final inspections · fire marshal final · health inspection · certificate application · punch · warranty documentation |
| You provide | Walkthrough attendance · equipment and furniture coordination · final payment |

**Billing:** Invoice for completed work; retainage per contract.

**Internal checklist:**
- Internal punch **before** calling the client walkthrough
- Final building, electrical, plumbing, mechanical inspections
- **Fire marshal final — sprinkler, alarm, egress, extinguishers, signage.**
  Usually the last inspection and the one that delays openings
- Health department final where food service is involved
- **Certificate of Occupancy where classification changed; Certificate of
  Completion where it did not.** Know which applies before the client announces
  an opening date
- Accessibility final verification against the stage 2 analysis
- Landlord walkthrough and sign-off (TI)
- Client walkthrough; punch list written, dated, resolved
- Warranty package, O&M manuals, equipment documentation, as-builts
- **Train the client's staff on systems they now operate** — HVAC controls,
  alarm panel, shutoffs. Skipping this generates warranty calls that aren't
  warranty issues

**Exit criteria:** **all finals passed including fire** · certificate issued ·
punch signed off · landlord sign-off (TI) · client trained

**Authority:** FBC-B 111 · Florida Fire Prevention Code

---

### Stage 8 — Closeout and warranty
*2–6 weeks* · `performedBy: covenant`

**Public summary:** Final paperwork, lien releases, and a warranty you can hold us to.

| Client-facing | |
|---|---|
| We handle | Final invoicing · lien releases · warranty documentation · post-occupancy follow-up |
| You provide | Final payment and retainage release |

**Billing:** Final invoice and retainage against complete lien release package.

**Internal checklist:**
- **Reconcile every Notice to Owner against a matching final lien release**
- **Contractor's final payment affidavit** — § 713.06
- Consent of surety where bonded
- As-built drawings and closeout documents delivered
- Warranty period start date recorded and diarised
- **Post-occupancy check at 30 days.** Commercial clients discover problems once
  they operate the space, and finding them yourself is far better than a
  complaint
- **Request a review and ask for referrals by name.** Commercial referrals travel
  through property managers, brokers, and other tenants — a satisfied commercial
  client is worth more introductions than a residential one
- Portfolio photos with written permission — **and check the lease; some
  landlords restrict photography or naming**

**Exit criteria:** lien releases complete · retainage released · closeout
documents delivered · warranty diarised · review requested

**Authority:** § 713.06

## Open items

1. **Tenant improvement or ground-up — which is most of the work?** The two
   branches carry very different effort, and knowing the mix determines what to
   build first.
2. **Do you carry the design professional, or does the client?** Commercial needs
   sealed drawings; who holds that contract changes stage 2 materially.
3. **Retainage and liquidated damages.** Do current commercial contracts include
   them? Both are common and both carry real exposure.
4. **Bonding capacity.** Any public or bonded work, or private only?
5. **Insurance limits.** Do current limits meet what commercial landlords and
   owners typically require, or has that been a barrier?
6. **Food service experience.** Restaurants add health department, grease, hood
   suppression, and a different inspection path. Worth knowing if that's a
   target market or one to decline.
7. **Verify accessibility, asbestos, and fire code requirements** against current
   rules before treating this as authoritative.
