# Website copy — stop describing, start converting

**Status:** plan. Ready to build on approval of the hero direction.
**Date:** 2026-08-01

---

## The diagnosis

Every section of the homepage describes **Covenant**. None address the person
reading it.

| Section | What it says | What a visitor thinks |
|---|---|---|
| Hero | "Building your vision with quality and precision" | Could be any contractor in Florida |
| Introduction | "We build your dreams… we specialize in creating exceptional spaces" | 60 words of filler, above the fold on a phone |
| Services | Five cards, all linking to one long page | A menu |
| Why choose us | Licensed · Full-service · Craftsmanship · Communication | Every competitor claims all four |
| Service areas | A list of towns | — |

That's the directory problem: the page is organised around **your internal
structure**, not around what a visitor is trying to do.

**The best thing on the page is buried.** The licence band — number,
classification, licensed since 2005, active through 2028, with a link to verify
on the state site — is real, checkable, and almost nobody else publishes it. It's
styled as a detail strip. It should be the argument.

---

## The position

A homeowner arriving here has a job to do and a fear. **The fear is bigger.**

Someone unlicensed. Someone who takes a deposit and disappears. After a storm,
someone from out of state who knocked yesterday.

> **Covenant is the contractor you can check before you let them in.**

Licensed since 2005, verifiable in thirty seconds on a state website, based
here, still here after the storm. That position is true, defensible, and already
sitting in the page unused.

## Homepage — section by section

### 1 · Hero

Three directions:

**A — recommended**
> # A Florida builder you can verify before you call.
> Licensed since 2005 — CBC1253676. Homes, kitchens, commercial and storm
> restoration across Vero Beach and the Treasure Coast.

**B — storm-weighted**
> # After the storm, everyone knocks. We've been here since 2005.
> Strong for restoration, weaker for a custom-home buyer.

**C — tagline-led**
> # We Deliver.
> Licensed Florida contractor since 2005. Verify the licence, then let's talk
> about your project.

**A works for all five services, is literally true, and invites the exact action
that wins the job — checking.**

Buttons: **Request an estimate** (primary) · **(772) 473-7115** beside it.
Nothing else competing.

One line under: *Vero Beach · Sebastian · Fort Pierce · Port St. Lucie ·
Fellsmere · Bilingual — hablamos español*

### 2 · Licence band — promoted

Move directly under the hero, given room. Number, classification, licensed
since, active through, verify link. This is the proof the headline promises;
it should be the second thing on the page, not the fifth.

### 3 · References — the review substitute

**There are no reviews on this site, and this is how we handle it honestly
without drawing a circle around the gap.**

> ## Don't take our word for it
> Most contractors send you to a review page. We'll give you a phone number.
>
> Ask and we'll connect you with recent clients on the Treasure Coast — talk to
> them directly and ask whatever you want.
>
> **Call (772) 473-7115** · **estimating@covenantbuilders.org**

Why this framing:

- **Never announce the absence.** "Working on getting reviews posted" makes a
  visitor think about reviews who wasn't, and reads apologetic — wrong note for
  a firm licensed 21 years.
- **References are the stronger signal for high-ticket work.** Someone spending
  $300k on a custom home, or rebuilding after a hurricane, would rather call two
  past clients than read star ratings. Star ratings matter more for pizza.
- **It's a position, not a consolation.** "We'll give you a phone number" is
  something competitors with 200 reviews cannot say.

**Before this ships:** get permission from the clients you'd name. Sending a
stranger to a past client who wasn't asked is a bad surprise for everyone. Two
or three willing references is plenty — Towne and Hastings are both closed.

**This section is temporary.** Once real Google reviews exist it becomes a
review strip, and the references line moves to a supporting sentence.

### 4 · Five reasons — the company set

From `selling-points-plan.md`. Replaces the current generic four. Licensed since
2005 · we pull our own permits · we're from here · hablamos español · one
contractor start to finish.

### 5 · What we build

Five short cards, each linking to its own service page per
`services-page-plan.md`. No process detail here.

### 6 · Real work

Three photos, honest captions. **Already good** — the captions describe what's
in the frame instead of inventing outcomes. Leave them alone.

### 7 · Close

One CTA, phone beside it.

### Cut

- **The "Introduction / We build your dreams" block.** 60 words of nothing,
  above the fold on mobile.
- **Service areas as a standalone section.** One line under the hero, plus the
  footer.

## Two structural notes

**"Investors" is in the main navigation.** On a site whose job is converting
homeowners that's a distraction, and it raises a question most visitors
shouldn't be asking. Move it to the footer.

**Conversion is now measurable.** The contact form writes into the CRM, so every
copy change shows up as jobs at the Lead milestone with source `website`. Record
the count before this ships so the comparison means something.

## The honest summary

Copy can make this site clearer, calmer and more confident. It cannot manufacture
social proof.

**Until real reviews exist, the site argues from credentials alone.** Credentials
are a strong argument — most competitors can't match 21 years and a verifiable
licence. Credentials plus five neighbours saying you did what you promised is a
much stronger one, and that's a build, not a rewrite.
